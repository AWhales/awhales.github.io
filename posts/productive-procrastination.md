I've been trying to get a 15 minute slice together in my spare time over the past couple months, but some days I find I'm not in the right headspace for level design or writing, so I find some mildly productive thing to procrastinate with.

Often that has been focused on tooling, writing scripts/automations that make the process of creating easier — one of the recent tools revolved around binding my level editor to Photoshop files instead of manually updating PNGs. Now I simply click 'edit' in my level to open Photoshop, make the alterations I want and save the PSD. This triggers a script that automatically bakes down the file into the foreground and background layer PNGs and updates the editor and game.

These tools definitely save time in the long run, but honestly sometimes I feel more tempted to make tools than actually work on the game, the satisfaction is so immediate and the reward is so obvious that it gives me a proper dopamine hit, something that is sometimes lacking in the actual game making marathon.

I've been trying to put a stop to this tooling brand of productive procrastination, but I've noticed it's started appearing in other areas instead.

For example, I recently got into the idea of using shaders to replace/enhance some hand drawn pixel art. It worked so well that I started thinking about all the other things I could replace with shaders, and started building things that I have no need for within the vertical slice.

Here was the seed of one idea, that I began doodling in a particularly long meeting, of how to form clouds from shaded circles with blend modes. If they were formed of circles they could be easily controlled to be interactive and dynamic — the player could walk through them and the clouds would simply move around them.

![Clouds from shaded circles](/assets/notes/cloud-example.png)

I got two full days into building and optimising this before I realised what I was doing — procrastinating again!

![Walking through shader clouds](/assets/notes/clouds.webm)

I do think they look good and I'll definitely use them now they exist, but I think it's highlighted for me how easy it is to drift on a solo project because at the end of the day I am the only person I answer to.
